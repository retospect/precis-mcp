## `fi189542` — the source contradicts itself, so don't "fix" the claim

The claim gives opening angles of approximately 19°, 39°, 60°, **85°** and 113°.
The same passage that supplies them also states the governing relation
`sin(θ/2) = 1 − P/6`. At P = 2 that yields **83.6°**, not 85° — the other four
values match the formula to under half a degree. Ref 783 is internally
inconsistent.

This is corroborated from inside the corpus, independent of ref 783: `pc972022`
(ref 5828, ord 62) states θ = 2·arcsin(1 − n/6) directly.

**Do not unilaterally rewrite the claim to 83.6°.** The hub faithfully reports
what its source says; editing the number would make the claim diverge from its
own grounding and silently launder someone else's error into an apparently
clean one. The honest move is to attach `pc972022` to `fi189542` as a
`contradicts` edge — the corpus then carries the inconsistency explicitly — and
let the primary adjudicate. That primary is Krishnan et al. 1997
(`10.1038/41284`), paywalled and not in corpus.

## Handling notes for whoever picks this up

**`unacquirable_note=` is the wrong tool here** — an earlier version of this
file recommended it, incorrectly. Per `precis-finding-help` it declares that a
*print-only / undigitized* source is legitimately citeable despite no digital
copy being obtainable, and `unacquirable_mode='abstract'|'vouched'` asserts that
someone read the abstract, or vouches for the claim, on that basis. Neither
holds: Krishnan 1997 and Iijima 1999 are digitized and obtainable with
institutional access, and nobody has read either. Using it would fabricate an
assertion no one made — which is exactly what the skill warns against.

The correct route for the two paywalled primaries is acquisition mode:
`put(kind='paper', doi=…)` stubs, or a `wants=`/`provenance=` mint, which lands
them in the `/drive` hand-download queue rather than asserting anything about
the claims.

Two mechanical gotchas, both paid for once already:

- `edit(kind='finding', …)` accepts **exactly one of** `pick_candidate=` |
  `title=` | `unacquirable_note=`. It supports neither find-replace nor
  `dry_run`. So the `fi189542` angle correction and its unacquirable note are
  necessarily two separate calls, and the correction must pass the **full**
  replacement claim string.
- `refs.title` is capped at 200 characters and only mirrors the claim; the
  authoritative text is the `ord=0` chunk. A retitle whose change falls past
  character 200 produces before/after echo lines that look identical. Verify
  against the chunk, not the echo.

A retitle goes through `hub.py::refine_claim_sentence`, which does a
DELETE+INSERT of the `ord=0` chunk so the embedding and summary cascades re-run,
and keeps the old `pub_id` as an alias so existing cites still resolve.

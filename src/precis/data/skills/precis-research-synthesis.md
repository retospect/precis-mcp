# precis-research-synthesis — compose an answer from evidence

Use for combining findings into an explanation, comparison, model or hypothesis
that answers a research question. Start from the sources and structured claims
produced by [[precis-research-analysis]], or reconstruct the missing analysis.
A synthesis request alone returns an answer and proposed relationships;
requested persistence uses the existing evidence and memory workflows.

## Build a coherent answer

Restate the question, the comparison being made and the conditions under which
an answer would hold. Read the relevant claim records, their evidence and their
qualifications; preserve the distinction between a source's assertion and
judged support. [[precis-research-help]] supplies the source quality bar;
[[precis-citation-help]] supplies the claim-level citation procedure.

Group claims by the distinctions that affect the answer: mechanism, setup,
method, operational definition, time or scale. Combine compatible results
without silently transferring them to a different regime. Compare quantities
only where their definitions and conditions permit it. Shared vocabulary or
repeated citation of one original experiment does not establish independent
corroboration. Trace support to the passages that actually report it.

Propose an organization that explains how the claims relate. For every material
conclusion identify its premises, the additional inference if any, and the
condition or assumption that licenses that step. Test the account against the
strongest relevant counterevidence and a plausible alternative explanation.
Keep competing explanations separate when the available evidence cannot choose
between them. Coherence alone does not settle a claim.

Revisit analysis when the proposed account exposes a conflated concept, a scope
mismatch or an unsupported bridge between sources. Retrieve the missing passage
or revise the question before polishing the narrative. Report a consequential
gap explicitly when the evidence cannot resolve it. [[precis-review-paper-help]]
can challenge the resulting scientific argument.

## Separate synthesis from new evidence

A conclusion not stated in a grounding passage remains your inference. Cite
each source only for the premise it supplies. Describe the extra step and its
uncertainty instead of attributing the whole conclusion to those sources.
For a cross-paper hypothesis, follow [[precis-read-for-question]]'s cross-paper
inference procedure and [[precis-nanopub-help]]: preserve the motivation,
supporting handles and proposed test. Do not turn a plausible mechanism into a
grounded claim by attaching citations to its component facts.

For a contestable reasoning step worth inspecting, [[precis-argument-help]]
records the premises, rule, warrant and conclusion. That record exposes the
reasoning; it does not prove it. Keep hypotheses and conditional conclusions
labelled as such throughout the account.

## Deliver and persist the result

Return the answer with its applicability, the claim/source handles supporting
each substantive conclusion, the added inference steps, counterevidence and
remaining questions. Preserve access to the atomic claims from any summary.
The summary provides organization; it need not repeat every claim body.

For requested graph changes, reuse existing claim identities and precise scope.
[[precis-taproot-mint-help]] owns evidence attachment and claim refinement;
[[precis-make-memory]] owns reconciliation of durable notes. Keep proposed
changes distinct from completed writes, and read back written relationships.

## A new explanation from two sources

Paper A observes a long-lived intermediate under one solvent. Paper B shows
that this intermediate reacts rapidly with a proposed substrate under another
solvent. Together they motivate a pathway hypothesis. Neither source establishes
that the whole pathway operates under one shared setup. Preserve both premises,
name the solvent-transfer assumption and propose the experiment that would test
the bridge. Retain the hypothesis even if the final answer remains uncertain.

If "stable" means activity retention in one source and thermodynamic stability
in another, a single stability ranking would erase the distinction. Return to
analysis and compose an answer with both meanings visible.
